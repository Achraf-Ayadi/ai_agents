Chosen company for investment:
- Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSMC)

Why TSMC was chosen:
- Essential AI infrastructure role: TSMC is the leading semiconductor foundry and a critical supplier for AI chips and advanced process nodes. In an era of accelerating AI deployment, the reliability and scalability of a core manufacturing partner are foundational to AI hardware ecosystems.
- Momentum in advanced nodes: The research notes highlight strong momentum in N3 and N4/N5 nodes, which are key to enabling next-generation AI accelerators. This positioning supports durable growth as demand for cutting-edge chips remains robust.
- Market positioning and long-term growth: TSMC is positioned to benefit from the AI surge and the growing need for advanced-node capacity, with long-term revenue potential driven by sustained AI infrastructure demand.
- Relative risk considerations: While TSMC faces exposure to capex cycles and geopolitical risk, its role as an essential supplier and its scale provide resilience through multiple cycles and customers.

Concerns and risks to monitor:
- Capex cycles: The company’s revenue is tied to spending on manufacturing capacity, which can be cyclical.
- Geopolitical risk: Concentration of production in politically sensitive regions introduces potential supply and policy risks.

Companies not selected and why:
1) NVIDIA
- Reason not selected: NVIDIA is a clear leader in AI chips with strong data-center revenue potential and dominant position in GPUs. However, the investment case here is less about foundational manufacturing capacity and more about product cycles and software ecosystem dynamics. While compelling, it is one step removed from the broad, long-term AI infrastructure backbone that TSMC represents as a foundry supplier.

2) Disney
- Reason not selected: Disney is pursuing AI-driven content development (with Adobe) and has visible AI-related growth potential in media production. Yet, this is a different investment thesis focused on content, media IP, and regulatory/IP considerations rather than a foundational semiconductors supplier. The exposure to regulatory/IP risk and the uncertain monetization of AI initiatives make it a less direct fit for a “best investment in AI infrastructure” thesis.

3) ByteDance
- Reason not selected: ByteDance has AI tool developments and IP considerations, but the note about IP safeguards after a Disney threat signals regulatory and competitive risk in the AI video space. Given the regulatory landscape and the competitive environment, it’s a higher-risk pick relative to TSMC.

4) LangChain
- Reason not selected: LangChain achieved a $3B valuation with a $300M Series C, signaling strong investor confidence in AI tooling and enterprise adoption potential. However, as a (primarily software/infrastructure) startup at a relatively early stage, it carries higher venture-style risk and less visible, diversified, long-term cash flow compared to a dominant semiconductor foundry with entrenched industry positioning.

5) Micron Technology
- Reason not selected: Micron is tied to AI memory demand cycles (DRAM/HBM) and could benefit from AI-driven memory demand. Nonetheless, the research notes emphasize cyclical risks and inventory dynamics in memory markets, which can introduce greater volatility and downside risk relative to the more structurally advantaged position of a leading foundry.

Among these, TSMC offers the most defensible, long-term, infrastructure-focused investment thesis based on its essential role in AI chip supply, leadership in advanced nodes, and scalable growth potential, even as it carries cyclical and geopolitical risks.
